# Changelog

Short notes on what changed in LiNaK. Newest first.

## Latest

### Fixed
- **Spectrum line heights.** Both old spectrum pages scaled every line's peak to its f whatever its width: a stick plot drawn with widths. Physically a line's area is π r_e c f, so a Doppler peak scales as f·λ and a natural one as f/Γ; UV against IR lines and broad against narrow lines were wrong. The broadenings were also shown one at a time instead of as one Voigt profile. The Spectra stage's "absorption" bar used every line in the table, excited lower levels included, as if every level were populated, and its "emission" bar was weighted by the absorption f instead of N_u A hν. Lines with ΔE at the table's 1e-6 eV rounding step (Rb had a "line" at 1.24 m) are now left out. All fixed in the new Spectrum page below.
- **Stages crawled when the CPU was shared.** numpy's BLAS started 4–6 threads per stage on a 20-core Windows PC, which gave no speed-up on LiNaK's few-hundred-row matrices (DHF Na: 31 s on the default threads, 26 s on one). Under a full test run those spinning threads slowed the fitted DHF runs past the tests' 15-minute timeout (25–40 s alone). LiNaK now sets one BLAS thread per process (`OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS`, `MKL_NUM_THREADS`, `OMP_NUM_THREADS`) unless you set them yourself; results are unchanged.
- **Breit–Rabi curves swapped levels at every crossing.** All eigenvalues were sorted together at each field and curve k kept the zero-field label of the k-th lowest level, so wherever two levels of different m_F crossed (22 times below 100 G for Na 3p3/2, throughout the Paschen–Back region of every excited state) the colour and the |F, m_F⟩ label jumped to another level. The Hamiltonian is now solved block by block in m_F, which is exact at every field: each curve is one level and keeps its labels.
- **g_J used g_S = 2.** The Landé formula took the electron spin g factor as 2, so ground-state g_J was 2.0000 instead of ≈2.0023 and every Zeeman slope was 0.1% small (3p3/2: 1.3333 vs measured 1.3341). It now uses g_S = 2.00231930436 and the reduced-mass g_L = 1 − m_e/M, and the bundled constants give the measured ground-state g_J (Na 2.0022960, K 2.002295, Rb 2.00233113, Cs 2.002542; Steck, Vanden Bout et al. Phys. Rev. 165, 88 (1968)).
- **g_I ignored diamagnetic shielding.** The nuclear Zeeman term used the bare μ_I/I from the IAEA table. Inside the atom the electron cloud shields the nucleus (0.4% for Rb, 0.6% for Cs), which moves every field-insensitive point: the 87Rb |1,−1⟩ ↔ |2,+1⟩ magic field came out at 3.2418 G instead of the measured 3.228917 G. The constants files now carry the measured atomic g_I′ (Arimondo, Inguscio & Violino 1977, `_gI_prime`) for Na, 39K, 41K, 85Rb, 87Rb and Cs, used when present; the stage prints which g_I it used. If your data folder has older copies of `<El>_hf_constants.json`, the stage says so: delete them to pick up the measured values.
- **Hyperfine level diagram:** "E1" connectors were drawn between any two consecutive states, including 3p1/2 → 3p3/2 (same parity, not an E1 line); the Zeeman page printed half-integer F and m_F rounded (210Fr, I = 6, showed F = 6 for 11/2).
- **Merged-J levels in the transitions list:** NIST lists the f and g levels from n = 12 as one level (`5/2,7/2`, `7/2,9/2`), and the transitions stage tested the ΔJ rule with the first J only, dropping f → g9/2 lines (for Na, 18 lines between f n = 12 to 20 and 18g9/2 or 6g9/2). Every J of a merged level is now tested. Only Na was affected (Li, K, Rb, Cs and Fr rebuild to identical lists). Na's bundled transitions and lifetimes files are regenerated: the 18g9/2 lifetime drops 12% (it lacked decay channels its 18g7/2 partner had), the f5/2 lifetimes n = 12 to 20 change by under 0.2%. Rerun Transitions, then Lifetimes, Polarizability and Blackbody for your own species data.

### New
- **Spectrum page** (Visualise > Spectrum, `--pipeline spectra`, `plots/<El>/<El>_spectrum.html`): one tabbed page replaces the Grotrian stage's `<El>_spectrum_levels.html` and the Spectra stage's `<El>_spectra.html`. Every line is a Voigt profile with area π r_e c f: Doppler at the gas temperature with an optional instrument Gaussian, the natural width (1/τ_u + 1/τ_l)/2π and an optional pressure or laser width. Lines narrower than a plotted point keep their area in it, and zooming recomputes the spectrum, so line shapes resolve at any range. Tabs:
  - **Absorption:** cross-section per atom with Boltzmann lower-level populations and stimulated emission; optical depth or transmission of a vapour cell whose density follows the vapour pressure (Alcock, Itkin & Horrigan 1984; Rb at 25 °C 1.29×10¹⁰ cm⁻³), or a density you set; the refractive index n − 1 from the same Faddeeva function.
  - **Emission:** optically thin, N_u A hν with upper levels populated at an excitation temperature (T → ∞ gives equal population per sublevel).
  - **D lines:** D1 and D2 resolved into hyperfine components, with the natural isotope mix (⁶Li/⁷Li, ³⁹K/⁴¹K, ⁸⁵Rb/⁸⁷Rb) and measured isotope shifts (Das & Natarajan 2007, Falke et al. 2006, Banerjee et al. 2003). Shown Doppler-broadened through the cell, or as saturated absorption with Lamb dips and crossovers (positions and widths exact; heights from the weak-saturation estimate without optical pumping).
  - **Curve of growth:** equivalent width against column density, with the thin and damping asymptotes and this cell marked.
  - **Strip:** white light through the vapour and the emission as colour bands, either as a line pattern or physically at a chosen resolution.
  - **Lines:** air and vacuum wavelengths, wavenumber, f, Δf, A, g, source, natural and Doppler widths, peak cross-section; searchable, sortable, CSV.

  The x axis switches between vacuum and air wavelength, cm⁻¹, eV and THz. Air wavelengths use standard air (NIST's formula), above 200 nm. Each tab can be left out (`--no-tab-absorption` … `--no-tab-lines`). The stage reads the lifetimes data only; the D-line tab also reads `<El>_hf_constants.json`. The transitions-only fallback (every f = 1) and the hard-coded D-line fallback are gone, and `--transitions-file` is ignored. For ions, the ground state is the level every other one lies above (Na⁺ 2p⁶; the metastable 3s ³P°₂ was a candidate). The Grotrian stage writes the Grotrian page only; its `--plots` option is still accepted. Checks: Na D2 air 588.99509 nm (NIST 588.9950954); Rb and Cs vapour pressure at 25 °C within 2% of Steck; Cs D2 F=4 → F′=5 at −3757.886 MHz from the centroid (Steck); the curve of growth's thin and damping limits. The browser's physics is tested against the Python (scipy's Faddeeva function) in node. The old pages are no longer written: delete `<El>_spectrum_levels.html` and `<El>_spectra.html` from your plots folder.
- **Hyperfine page** (Hyperfine / Zeeman stage, `plots/<El>/<El>_hyperfine.html`): one tabbed page in the style of the Grotrian, Polarizability and Blackbody pages (rail, theme, Export to SVG, PNG, PDF, CSV) replaces `<El>_hyperfine.html` and `<El>_zeeman.html`. Tabs: **Levels** (each state on its own frequency scale, F levels with their m_F sublevels and g_F, the clock interval), **Breit–Rabi** (several states side by side, curves coloured by m_F or F, energy or dE/dB in μ_B, a weak-field (linear) overlay, high-field |m_J, m_I⟩ labels, a field cursor with a table of every level's energy, slope and low/high-field-seeker status, and the |m_J, m_I⟩ composition of a clicked level vs B), **Transitions** (every ground-state ΔF = ±1 microwave line, single photon and two-photon, as ν − ν₀ vs B with its linear and quadratic Zeeman coefficients and its field-insensitive points; the Zeeman-resolved D lines vs B and as a σ+/π/σ− spectrum at the cursor field with an adjustable linewidth; the zero-field strengths S_FF′) and **Constants** (nuclear data, A, B, C, g_J and g_I with their sources). Field units G, mT, T and frequency units kHz, MHz, GHz switch on the page. Checks: 87Rb and 133Cs clock shifts 575.15 and 427.45 Hz/G² (Steck), 87Rb magic field 3.2289 G (measured 3.228917 G), 87Rb D2 S_FF′ = 7/10, 1/4, 1/20 and 5/12, 5/12, 1/6. The bundled pages are regenerated and the Zeeman pages removed; rerun the stage for your own species.
- **DHF + core polarisation stage** (Structure models > DHF + core polarisation, `--pipeline dhf`): a relativistic one-valence model independent of ORCA. Dirac–Hartree–Fock core in a B-spline basis with a finite nucleus, plus a core-polarisation potential whose cutoff is fitted to the NIST ionization energy (and, by default, the lowest d3/2 and d5/2 levels; `--no-fit-d` turns that off). It writes `data/<El>_dhf.json` with levels, the E1 network (reduced dipoles, f, A), lifetimes, α(0) and α(ω) (`--omega`), j-resolved quantum defects and a comparison with NIST. Accuracy (Na to Fr): D lines within 0.6%, D-line f within 0.4%, α(0) within 1.3%. `--ip` (cm⁻¹) replaces the NIST ionization energy, so the stage runs without NIST files (no d fit, no comparison then). `--n-extra` sets how far up the levels and lines go (n_valence + N). The "Quantum chemistry" sidebar group is renamed "Structure models" (ORCA, DHF + core polarisation, Brueckner).
- **Brueckner stage** (Structure models > Brueckner (DHF + Σ2), `--pipeline brueckner`): an energy-dependent core-polarisation model next to the DHF + core-polarisation stage, with its own output `data/<El>_brueckner.json` (same layout as `<El>_dhf.json`: levels, E1 network, lifetimes, α(0), α(ω) with `--omega`, quantum defects, NIST comparison). Levels: frozen-core DHF plus the second-order correlation potential Σ(2)(E) (core polarisation including its non-adiabatic part, plus the exchange term; Johnson et al., arXiv:physics/0310141), solved self-consistently per level. Dipoles: between the Brueckner orbitals, dressed by the core's RPA response at the transition frequency; the core polarisability comes from the same RPA (Li+ 0.1894 … Cs+ 15.82 a.u., matching relativistic RPA to 4 digits), so α needs no table. Fit to NIST (default, tick "Scale Σ2 to NIST"): one scale factor per l, j fitted to the lowest NIST level (s: the ionization energy); an l, j with no NIST level borrows the mean of the nearest l. Every other level is a prediction: Ca+ 4d +0.10% (DHF + core polarisation +0.83%), Sr+ 5d +0.06% (+0.51%), Ba+ 6d +0.11% (+0.7%), Ba+ 4f +0.24%/+0.41% (+4.8%), Na–Cs higher s/p/d within 0.01–0.02%. Lowest-p lifetimes within 0.5% of precision data for Na–Fr, Ca+, Sr+, Ba+ (Cs 6p −0.07%/+0.10%, Fr 7p1/2 +0.07%, Ba+ 6p1/2 +0.14% where DHF + core polarisation gives +1.7%); Cs 6s–6p1/2 4.4993 a.u. (measured 4.5057). `--unscaled` fits nothing: a pure ab initio run that needs no NIST data (s, p levels 1–3% off, inner d up to 28%). `--lmax` sets the virtual orbitals in Σ(2) (default 5). Weak, cancelling lines (Cs 6s–7p) are 4–7% low: third-order corrections are not included, and the DHF + core-polarisation stage does better there. The first run takes 1–8 minutes per species; Σ is kept in `data/<El>_brueckner_cache.npz`, so reruns take seconds to a minute. Checked against brute-force m-scheme sums (Σ(2) and the RPA vertex), Ginges et al. (arXiv:1603.09116, Cs Σ(2) energies) and Roberts et al. (arXiv:2211.11134, Cs DHF+RPA).
- **DHF stage: p levels and the d series as inputs** (default; `--no-fit-p`, `--d-one-level` restore the old behaviour; GUI ticks "No p-level fit", "Fit lowest d only"). The core-polarisation cutoffs of p1/2 and p3/2 are now fitted to the lowest p levels, and the d channel to two NIST d levels (the lowest and the highest pair up to n_valence + 8) through its cutoff and a factor on α_core (the core polarisability a d electron sees is smaller: 0.95 Na, 0.82 K, 0.64–0.65 Cs). Against precision data, the lowest-p lifetimes go from −1.0 to −1.2% to within ±0.3% for K, Rb, Cs (Ca+ 4p1/2 −2.0% → −0.05%), α(0) for Cs −0.9% → −0.5%, Fr −1.3% → −0.8%, and the d series is fixed: Cs 6d +0.20% → −0.06%, K 4d +0.05% → −0.02%, d levels from n_valence + 2 within 0.005%, n = 25 d quantum defects within 0.002 (was 0.013–0.015 low). Fine structure of the fitted p levels is exact (it was +39% Be+, +8% Na). The D lines are then inputs, not predictions. Where the lowest d is an inner (n−1)d that cannot share a potential with the nd series (Ca+ 3d, Sr+ 4d, Ba+ 5d: the α factor would be 0.06–0.2), the stage keeps the lowest-d fit and records why in `inputs.d_fit_note`. A static quadrupole core-polarisation term was tested and made every unfitted level worse, so it is not used.
- **DHF stage for one-valence ions** (`--pipeline dhf Ca_c1`; also Be_c1, Mg_c1, Sr_c1, Ba_c1): the same model with an M2+ core, so Rydberg levels converge on the next limit (Z_ion = 2 sets n* and the quantum defects). Without a NIST export for the ion, the NIST Handbook limit and lowest d levels built into the stage are used (`--ip` and a user export take precedence); core polarisabilities Be2+ 0.0519, Mg2+ 0.48, Ca2+ 3.284, Sr2+ 5.748, Ba2+ 10.426 a.u. Against NIST: resonance lines Mg+ ±0.01%, Be+ +0.4%, Sr+ +0.5%, Ca+ +0.6%, Ba+ +0.6–0.9%, higher s/p/d levels mostly 0.01–0.2%; α(0) Be+ 24.4, Mg+ 35.0, Ca+ 75.6, Sr+ 90.6, Ba+ 122.3 a.u. (references 24.5, 35.0, 75.5, 90.2–91.3, 121–124); 4p/6p lifetimes of Ca+ and Ba+ within 1–2% of the precision measurements. Ba+ 4f is 4–5% off (its 4f orbital half-collapses into the core). `--all` now includes the five ions. Neutral results are unchanged.
- **Structure-model f-values in Lifetimes and Polarizability** (`--model-f dhf` or `--model-f brueckner`, `--model-file`; "Model f-values" and "Model data" in both stages; `--dhf` and `--dhf-file` still work as the old spelling of `--model-f dhf`): lines with no NIST or literature value take their f from `<El>_dhf.json` or `<El>_brueckner.json` (the model's dipole at the line's NIST energy) instead of ORCA, Numerov or Coulomb, with a 5% uncertainty and source "DHF" or "Brueckner" (purple and violet in the spectra). Measured values are never replaced. Two kinds of line are left to the other sources: ground ns → np beyond the resonance doublet (the Cooper-minimum series, where DHF is off by 5–20% and more for Cs p1/2; NIST and the extrapolator cover it) and lines whose radial integral nearly cancels. DHF only reaches as high as it was run: for Na, NIST covers n ≈ 20, so run the DHF stage with `--n-extra 27` (n = 30) to replace Numerov above that. Off by default: no existing output changes.
- **Rydberg shifts, series repair (Blackbody stage):** the Na g series no longer jumps. Three causes were fixed: level energies the quantum-defect model built are used at the formula's value instead of the 1e-6 eV rounding (the f-g gap at n = 33 is the size of that step); f-g pairs lost when two rounded energies tied (n = 34, 35, 41, 42, 44, 45) are restored; and an h (l = 5) series from the same model supplies the upward partners g states lacked (the g dynamic shift was -460 to +1400 Hz at n = 25 to 45 and now runs from 2342 to 2396 Hz, towards the 2417 Hz Farley-Wing limit; all g states now close the f-sum rule). `l = h` is added to the angular-momentum table. The data files are not changed; the repair is made when the shifts are computed. Rerun the Blackbody stage to get it. The same repair restores f(5/2,7/2) to 18g9/2 and 6g9/2 pairs that the transitions list drops for NIST's merged-J f and g levels (n = 12 to 20): the 17f5/2 dynamic shift was 490 Hz and is now 2105 Hz, and the f series is smooth.
- **Rydberg shifts, `static_valid`:** each state in `<El>_blackbody.json` now says whether the static Itano formula applies: false where the coupling to a near-degenerate partner exceeds 0.3 of its gap (second-order theory fails) or where over 10% of α(0) comes from partners below k_B T. Most Rydberg states fail, which is the reason the dynamic shift is the one to use there. The Blackbody page marks them and gives the reason; the numbers stay in the file.
- **Blackbody page, written by the Blackbody stage:** the stage's Generate plot tick now writes one tabbed page, `plots/<El>/<El>_blackbody.html`, next to its data; there is no separate Visualise stage and the old per-plot pages (shifts, rates, photoionization, alpha) and the hub are removed. Tabs: Shifts vs T (static and dynamic shift of the ground, excited and difference, with the correction as a percent of the static shift), Depopulation (Γ_BBR or τ_eff against n, with a Γ(T) curve for any state you click and a table of the channels behind each rate), Photoionization (direct PI, field ionization at a typed field, mixing PI and total ion rate; click a legend entry to hide that rate) and Rydberg shifts. Tick which tabs to include on the stage (`--no-tab-shifts`, `--no-tab-depopulation`, `--no-tab-photoionization`, `--no-tab-rydberg`); units, temperature range and field are options (`--plot-units`, `--plot-t-min`, `--plot-t-max`, `--field`). Temperature, units, n range and field also change live in the page. A tab whose data the run skipped is left out with a message. The reference plots for Li, Na, K, Rb, Cs and Fr are regenerated.
- **Polarizability page** (Visualise > Polarizability page, `--pipeline polarizability_page`): polarizability, magic wavelengths and optical tweezer on one tabbed page, written to `plots/<El>/<El>_response.html`. α(ω) is summed in the browser from the stored transitions, so units (a.u., Hz per W/cm², µK per kW/cm²), wavelength range, trap intensity and cursor wavelength change live; a searchable, paged table shows which lines make up α at the cursor, and magic wavelengths are marked by robustness (circle, diamond, square). The Polarizability and Optical tweezer stages now write data only (their separate plots and the Generate plot tick are gone from the GUI; the command line keeps `--no-plot`). Tick which tabs to build: Polarizability, Magic wavelengths (the differential polarizability Δα and the magic wavelengths), Optical tweezer. Input options: polarizability and lifetimes data; output option: output directory (and Save as).
- **License:** LiNaK is now released under GPL-3.0-or-later (was MIT). `LICENSE`, `pyproject.toml` and `CITATION.cff` are updated.
- **Grotrian diagram rebuilt:** one page with levels in columns by orbital l, J fine-structure bars, a filter rail (region, level class, f source, f grade, n, wavelength, minimum f, ground or excited lower level), line weight by f, click / shift-click / isolate, a matching-transitions table, right-click **View more** (all data for the transition, including the extrapolation model f with its 68% and 95% bands), ORCA overlay markers at their exact energies (no residual panel), light and dark theme, and the Plotly toolbar's camera button for an SVG export. The excited-to-excited Grotrian (Plot 3, `*_ee_grotrian.html`) and `--plot3-choice` are gone: the Excited → excited tick covers it. `--plots` is now 1 or 2.
- **Grotrian diagram:** an "Extra data to overlay" field takes several ORCA-format files (separated by ;, or Add files...) and draws each as its own overlay.
- **NIST f-value scale** table (Tables and variables > Lifetimes): Fr 7d3/2 and 7d5/2 start ticked. The NIST 7p-7d lines give 53.0 and 77.0 ns; with the scale the lifetimes come out at the measured 73.6 and 67.7 ns, and the lifetimes, polarizability and blackbody stages all use the scaled f-values. Untick the rows to get the NIST values back (rerun the stages). The two measured lifetimes are also in the known lifetimes, as a check.
- **Compare elements** now shows the table live in the viewer when it finishes: sortable columns grouped in sections (Atom, D1, D2, Natural, Doppler, Laser cooling), Remove selected, a temperature box that recomputes the Doppler columns, an Add box for more elements (they are added to the saved table, not replacing it), Export CSV, and Raw JSON. The HTML page is optional: tick **Generate HTML**.
- **Run > Tests...** opens a window to run all, selected or failed tests, with progress and details.
- **Edit > Preferences > Tables and variables** shows the built-in constants and data files, with units, and lets you edit them. Edits are saved in the `tables/` folder of the output folder, and Restore Defaults brings the originals back.
- **Add...** and **Remove** in Tables and variables for the open lists (known lifetimes, element masses, abundances, core polarizabilities, A_L and quantum-defect differences, precision f-values, and whole new elements in the D-line table). New rows are checked for typos and duplicates, can carry a source note, and never replace a built-in row.
- **Edit > Preferences > Extrapolator** page for the extrapolation settings.
- **Override** column for known lifetimes: tick a row (or Override all) to use that lifetime instead of the computed one in the output and plots. The computed value is kept in the JSON.
- **Hyperfine constants** can be entered in the set-up dialog (mass number, then A, B, C for each state).
- **Properties...** (right-click a species, or Edit > Species properties...) shows that element's built-in values and data files in one place, flags what is missing, and lets you add or edit it.
- **New species:** setting an atom with no bundled data (for example Mg) opens a dialog to import its NIST levels and lines and type its numbers, and shows which stages that enables.
- **Ctrl+F** find bar in the JSON viewer, the data editor and the tables page (F3 / Shift+F3 for next / previous).
- **Fr** now has extrapolated ns→np oscillator strengths, with its own derived parameters (`tools/fr_derivation/`).
- Extrapolation of ns→np oscillator strengths with a calibrated Bayesian ridge model, user anchors and an optional threshold anchor.

### Interface
- **Grotrian diagram without ORCA.** The stage no longer stops when there is no ORCA data: the levels and lines come from the Rydberg, Transitions and Lifetimes stages, and the TD-DFT / SOC levels are drawn over them only when present. It stops only when there are no levels at all.
- **Optical tweezer moved up** in Atomic response, right after Polarizability: it is the stage that uses α(ω) directly. Blackbody now comes third. Neither stage needs the other, so nothing about running them changes.
- Options of the DHF + core polarisation and Brueckner stages are grouped into Model, Fit to NIST, Input and Output; the fit options are now ticks that say what they do ("Fit p levels", "Fit d levels", "Second d level", "Scale Σ2 to NIST") instead of "No ..." ticks. Stage options can name their section (`section=` in the stage table).
- Stage options are grouped into Input, Output and Options sections, with path pickers and grey default hints.
- Compare, transitions and lifetimes inputs reworked; combo boxes open downward.
- Extrapolation dialog: wider plot by default, the example in Edit datasets is now a template you can copy, unreliable rows are shown unticked and disabled.
- Shorter hover texts everywhere.
- Fixes for Windows test failures (viewer stack, paths).

### Physics fixes
- Rydberg: neutral atoms that are not alkalis always use the NIST levels (no quantum-defect model).
- Delete species can also remove the NIST files you imported for it.
- Scattering rate: an element you added is no longer dropped from the list.
- Lifetimes: A coefficients were counted twice; fine-structure 6j factors; J-resolved NIST multiplets rebuilt; Ritz levels used; literature values for K, Rb and Cs anchors.
- Numerov: grid fix and a gap-dependent scale; Fr calibration fixed.
- Extrapolation: Fr NIST rows for 7s→np (n ≥ 8) are a plain 2:1 split and are excluded.
- Polarizability: uncertainty (sigma) now propagates correctly.
- Blackbody: guards against bad input.
- Hyperfine: Breit-Rabi and octupole terms corrected.
- Isotope default is chosen by natural abundance.
- Tweezer: scattering rate (Gamma) corrected.
- C6 (Tang) formula, Feshbach grouping, sigma Lorentzian, hybrid Coulomb f, clock transition, D-line table and nuclear data fixed.
- Plot fixes.

### Other
- README updated; tests added for the extrapolator, Fr, find bar, tables, test runner.
